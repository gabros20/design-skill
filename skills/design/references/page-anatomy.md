# Page anatomy

Purpose: Decide what a website page contains and in what order — the anatomy its visitor's job
implies, the macrostructure, the narrative and feeling curve, section compositions, repetition bans,
the SaaS deck and pricing, product and case-study pages, and the nav and footer.

Read when:
- Planning or restructuring a marketing, landing, product, pricing, portfolio or booking page: which
  sections, in what order, how it opens and how it ends.

Skip when:
- The look of the surface (dial, section craft, hero discipline, award tier) → surface-website.md, which
  loads with this file; product/app screens → surface-webapp.md; checkout and wizards → journeys.md.

Inputs:
- The visitor's job, the brief's facts and real material (prices, captures, logos with permission,
  testimonials), awareness of the arriving traffic, and earlier builds' fingerprints.

Produces:
- A section sequence with each section's composition, the feeling curve with its named peak, and the
  chrome — every section backed by material the brief actually supplies.

Numbers here are Restrained-lane defaults; the direction contract's dimensions override them; floors (marked by their basis) do not.

## Anatomy by visitor job

The shape follows the visitor's job, not the software-landing default (hero beside picture, figure
row, three steps, email band). Name the job, then place its action and native sections:
**subscribe** — field in or as the hero, a sample issue, the archive as an index · **book** — picker
(party, date, time) from nav and hero, repeated beside the menu or classes; hours and address ·
**visit** — today's hours and address up top; what's on as dated rows; getting there · **attend** —
tickets in the nav; date and place are the headline; lineup, schedule, venue · **buy** — the offer
(picture, options, price, action, one delivery line), often the hero · **try** — store buttons under
the head per platform; captured screens as named moments · **sign up** — start-free + terms line;
product at work, code sample, pricing, FAQ · **hire** — plain contact in nav and after the work; the
work as an index · **join** — first visit, sessions as a schedule · **listen** — the release with its
buttons. Where the job is a task, the first viewport carries it **in working form** (the route-and-dates
search, the open slots), not a link to it. **The close is the job's own action**; an email band closes
only where a list is native. Test: could this sequence sit under another business's name?

## Macrostructures and fingerprints

**Structural sameness is the AI fingerprint, not visual sameness.** Two briefs must yield different
*sites*, not colour-swaps of one skeleton, unless the contract names the category standard. **21 macrostructures** (each bundles heading placement + body
composition + divider + button voice + image treatment + reveal): Bento Grid · Long Document · Marquee
Hero · Stat-Led · Workbench · Conversational FAQ · Manifesto · Photographic · Quote-Led · Specimen ·
Catalogue · Letter · Index-First · Narrative Workflow · Split Studio · Feature Stack · Type Specimen ·
Portfolio Grid · Map/Diagram · Ecosystem Index · Component Playground. Vague brief → pick from the first
ten. **Specimen is not a default.** A Marquee Hero's moving strip carries only redundant content, with
a pause control (accessibility.md). Design the page first, then name its macrostructure — reaching
into the list first collapses it into the same few shapes.

**Domain → offer a trio of categorically different macros:** podcast → Photographic/Quote-Led/Letter ·
shop → Catalogue/Photographic/Bento · docs → Workbench/Long-Document/Component-Playground · SaaS-B2B →
Bento/Workbench/Stat-Led · agency → Portfolio-Grid/Split-Studio/Index-First · restaurant →
Photographic/Long-Document/Catalogue · fashion → Photographic/Catalogue/Marquee-Hero · fintech →
Stat-Led/Workbench/Long-Document · conference → Marquee-Hero/Manifesto/Photographic.

**Six structural axes** (heading placement · body composition · divider language · button voice · image
treatment · reveal); with the nav and the close counted too, no two pages share >3. Record each build's
fingerprint (macro, lane, palette, type pair, hero posture, nav/footer) in system memory and compare the
plan with **each** of the last three builds, not an average — an anti-drift check on unintended
sameness that breaks ties and never overrides brief fit; a repeat that fits is named in the contract. A recoloured or retuned effect is not a signature.

## Narrative: the question chain

A Persuade page answers the question chain; the **8 beats** are a checklist, not a sequence: 1 Hook · 2 Problem empathy · 3 USP · 4 Value props · 5 Proof points ·
6 How it works · 7 Stakes · 8 CTA. Walk top to bottom and mark where each answer lands: *What is this?
→ Is it for me? → Why better? → Can I trust it? → How does it work? → How much effort? → What next?*
Answering **early is also a defect** (pricing above the fold to someone still asking "what is this").
The hook is a question, not a summary; value props are benefits, not feature nouns. Every claim carries proof, beside the claim it supports.
Stakes come from consequence, not manufactured scarcity. CTA names the action ("Check fitment", not
"Learn more") and repeats down the page; one message per viewport.

**Weight by awareness** (marketing owns the stage; Schwartz's five levels): unaware → open on the
symptom or identity · problem-aware → the cost of the problem, with numbers · solution-aware → an honest
comparison of approaches · product-aware → proof, demo, objections · most-aware → risk reversal and
effort reduction. Paid traffic lands on a hero that repeats the ad's promise. **By vertical:**
portfolio — the work leads, the studio explains itself after; booking — desire before price, then
hours, location and access, with the chosen service, person and time visible to confirmation;
automation or AI for business — boundary, a verified problem metric, each capability as a workflow
with its permission, approval, audit and rollback, then evidence; the visitor leaves able to say what
it controls and how it undoes.

## Feeling curve

Beside the chain, write one line per section: the feeling, then what on screen causes it. Two
neighbours with one feeling: cut one. Name the peak as the sentence a visitor would repeat ("it's the
site where…", about what happened to them, never a device). **Feel check:** scroll the build once,
cold, writing one word per section before rereading the brief; where felt and intended disagree, the
page is wrong.

## Section compositions

Choose each section's composition from its content, by Persuade default 3–6 different ones a
page, not the same twice in a row (Read and Letter repeat on purpose): a **rail** for dated or browsable items (five or more, the next cut by the edge; lateral
travel reads as breadth, never a ranking) · a **switcher** for 2–5 real states (lunch/dinner,
monthly/yearly; first state visible without script) · an **index** for 6–20 items with facts in columns
· a **schedule** only for times a visitor plans around · an **offer** for the thing being bought.
**Mass, not air:** a heading and a paragraph are a head block, not a section, where the lane promises mass (layout-composition.md).

## Repetition defaults

- Persuade default: vary layout families (8 sections, ≥4 families; ≤2 consecutive image+text splits); Read, Letter and Long-Document repeat one on purpose.
- Label eyebrows that pass the eyebrow test (anti-defaults.md): at most 1 per 3 sections — `count ≤ ceil(sectionCount / 3)`; fact-bearing kickers (section, date, status) are uncapped.
- Split-header ("big headline left + small explainer right") is not a default; stack vertically.
- One corner-radius scale; alternating grounds is a lane device, not a ban.
- **Each fact once, where it does work:** a price, time or count in four or more sections is filler; a section's last line never restates its heading. A figure row uses only numbers the brief states outright — "365 mornings" from "every day" is invented — and each stat carries its denominator and source ("18.4% trial→paid · 4,120 teams, 2025"). Steps only for a process the visitor really goes through.

## The SaaS deck and pricing

**A deck, not a sequence.** Base cards are always honest: spec sheet (limits, formats, regions), a worked
example on real material, changelog with real dates, comparison against today's way (no scores),
pricing with the actual price, FAQ of real objections, one CTA strip, footer. **Material cards exist only
when the user supplied the material:** a hero capture, customer logos with permission, named features
each with a capture, verbatim testimonials with name, role and company (a wall of first names, five
stars and stock avatars reads fabricated). No material, no card — no "Trusted by" line. Deviate twice
per page: drop a base card, and promote one into the hero (a spec sheet as hero says "the numbers are
the pitch") or reorder a pair. Under five cards it is a strip, not a page.

**Pricing:** 3 cards with one recommended plan that differs in substance (marked by its button and one
word, never glow), a usage slider defaulting to the median customer, or persona-first paths. 3–6 limits
and 3–6 inclusions per plan, one CTA verb family, one value metric throughout, a jump to a comparison
grouped by what changes between tiers, ambiguous terms linked to their FAQ answer. A yearly view shows
the monthly equivalent and the amount billed — never a struck price alone — and any "months free" tag
matches the arithmetic. Align names, prices, buttons and features across tiers on shared rows
(subgrid), never by nudging. "Contact sales" on every tier reads as distrust of the buyer. A calculator
states its assumptions and formula. Mobile: stacked cards, featured tier first, never a sideways table.

## Product, case-study and vertical pages

**Product page:** the whole product leads the hero and at most two more sections; the rest show it in
use, parts close up, people with it, or a type moment. Every promise in a heading is pictured, or cut;
every colour offered appears at least once. **Case study:** title + one-line summary (what, for whom,
what changed) → meta (client, year, role, credits) → hero media → context → the idea → 3–5 captioned
craft frames → outcome (real results only, else what shipped) → next project.

## Navigation and footer

The conventional nav (wordmark left, links, primary action right) is the default for Persuade and
Operate; an edge-aligned bar, masthead, slab or search-led bar is a lane choice for Experience and
editorial surfaces. Footer: index columns for a real sitemap, else a mast, statement, colophon or
letter close. Sign-in defaults to a quiet text link, not a second button. One action keeps one label everywhere.
