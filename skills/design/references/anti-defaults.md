# Anti-defaults

Purpose: The canonical catalog of generated-look defaults — dated tells by layer, the current AI
cluster this model drifts into, the predict-then-refuse procedure, and the system-level fixes for a
templated result. Other references point here and never restate it.

Read when:
- A result reads generic, templated, or "AI-generated", or you are planning a surface and need the
  defaults it must refuse.
- Writing a refuse-list, running a deslop pass, or judging whether a choice is a reflex.

Skip when:
- Setting the register, lane, or concept → visual-direction.md (read it first on a new surface).
- Copy tells → content.md; motion tells → motion.md; a scored review → critique.md.

Inputs:
- The plan or the rendered surface, its register, and its brief.

Produces:
- A refuse-list with the replacement for each refused default, or a list of tells found.

## Using the catalog

A tell is an *unchosen* default: reflex, not law. The catalog is an anti-drift check on the agent's own
defaults — it never overrides the direction contract or a chosen convention (conventions.md). Rotated elements, stickers, tape, halftone, visible grids and overlapping type are lane
values in collage, zine, poster and Y2K lanes — tells only where no contract chose them. Refuse an
unchosen tell, then replace it; a ban with no substitute breeds the next tell. These are **brand-register**
tells; the serve register legitimately uses many (system fonts, familiar cards). Keep the dated and
exact values; they are the point. One hit proves little — clusters are the signal. Floor, Reflex and
Finish tiers (which tells may be waived on the record) are in critique.md.

**Richness goes on content, not chrome.** Generated screens smear colour and effect across the chrome
(glowing buttons, gradient fields, tinted borders, emoji labels); designed screens keep chrome quiet —
neutral surfaces, one accent, plain hairlines — and spend colour, material and detail on one or two
content moments.

## Predict, then decide

Before designing, write the page this prompt gets without trying and its obvious twist; neither ships
*by accident* — the predicted page ships only when the contract names it as the category-standard
choice. The default page: head-left/picture-right hero, three numbered steps, label-and-value rows
between hairlines, a closing band with a form, a dark footer under a huge wordmark, small grey
labels, a pale ground with one careful accent — two of these in one plan means the plan is the
template. The test: if someone could guess the aesthetic from the category, it must be a choice
the contract names, not a landing.

## Color

- Cream/sand/beige body background — the saturated AI default of 2026. Suspect the band **OKLCH L
  0.84–0.97, C < 0.06, hue 40–100**; tell token names when the contract never chose paper: `--paper --cream --sand --linen --parchment
  --bone --flour --wheat --ivory`.
- Premium-consumer palette (cookware/wellness/luxury/DTC) — banned defaults: backgrounds `#f5f1ea
  #f7f5f1 #fbf8f1 #efeae0 #ece6db #faf7f1 #e8dfcb`; accents `#b08947 #b6553a #9a2436 #9c6e2a
  #bc7c3a`; text `#1a1714 #1a1814`. Rotate to a different family (cold-luxury silver+chrome,
  forest+bone+amber, cobalt+cream, terracotta+slate); rotation breaks ties, never overrides fit.
- Purple→blue / indigo→violet gradients, on text or as accent ("the lila rule"); saturated diagonal
  gradients (purple→pink); gradient-filled hero headings; dark mode + single acid-green/vermilion
  glow. In a Restrained plan, one accent at saturation < 80%.
- A hue picked for the category (blue for software, green for health); **dev-tool dark** (blue-grey
  near-black ~`#0D1117`) reads as an editor screenshot — take the dark from the brand's world.
- An accent that collides with semantics (brand red meaning "new" beside red errors): move the hue or
  mark errors by shape.
- A single accent-coloured radial **spotlight** behind the headline or lockup, or a coloured glow
  shadow on dark — the stock backdrop, not the aurora technique (imagery-depth-decoration.md).

## Typography

Display oversizing, over-tight tracking and the reflex-reject font list: typography.md. Also: Inter body
everywhere (legit only in serve); one neutral grotesk in every role, display included; a
**system display face** (Impact, Arial Black, the platform sans) as an own-world display voice — source
and self-host the face; mono as a "technical" costume, or a mono face on every small label; accenting
one word of each headline (italic, bold or colour) as the only typographic idea.

## Layout and structure

- **Eyebrow test** — tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows appear on 55–95% of generations. Keep small type
  above a heading only when it carries a fact, destination or identifier (a date, filename, live
  status, linked announcement); delete it and reread — if only a name for what follows was lost, it
  was an eyebrow. The pill-chip above a hero headline counts. Replace, don't just delete: the heading
  names its subject in its first words, air above, a heavier lead-in, a rule or change of ground, a
  real `<ol>`, a caption below, a running head. Count cap: page-anatomy.md.
- Numbered section markers `01/02/03` as decoration; nested-card soup; icon-tile stacks (rounded
  squares, each one line-icon + label); **centered three-up identical feature cards**; split-header
  (big headline left, small explainer right); left-text/right-image hero as the first instinct.
- **Side-stripe cards:** a coloured `border-left/right` >1px on a *rounded* (≥4px) card, list item or
  alert. A square stripe is a legitimate callout, pull-quote or row marker.
- A heading nearer the previous section than its own content; a horizontal scroller that loses its
  end gutter; a desktop page stacked into mobile without re-ranking; marketing spacing on a dashboard;
  `→` appended to every link and button; labels shaped "WORD — fragment".

## Surfaces and effects

- Ghost card (`1px` border + `box-shadow` ≥16px blur on the *same* element); over-rounding
  (one large radius on every element regardless of hierarchy); hand-drawn / sketch **displacement** filters (static `feTurbulence` grain
  at ≤0.08 is the texture, not the tell); `repeating-linear-gradient` candy stripes.
- **Hard offset shadow** (zero blur) outside a world that chose neobrutalism; a **zero-offset coloured
  halo** — shadows need offset and blur.
- Hairline grid, dot-grid, blueprint or stripe backgrounds that mark no container, canvas, map or
  measurement; a texture passes only if the layout reads the same with it removed.
- A geometric clip-path or mask faking an organic edge (torn paper, silhouette) — use an alpha matte
  or cutout. A hero-sized SVG/CSS **drawing of the subject** (object, person, product, place) —
  imagery-depth-decoration.md tier B.

## Production tells

Refuse by default: version labels in hero (`V0.6`/`BETA`); "Brand · No.01" sub-eyebrows;
section-number eyebrows (`00/INDEX`, `001·Capabilities`); `01/4` pagination on tiles; middle-dot `·`
more than once per line; decorative status dots on every list/nav item, or a **pulsing "live" dot on
static data**; a blinking caret on text nobody can edit; `<br>`-broken italicized headlines; vertical
rotated text; crosshair/hairline grid decoration; **div-based fake product UI / terminal / dashboard in
the hero (the #1 tell)** — the honest route is product-visuals.md; fake version footers; "Quietly
trusted by"; poetic sidebar labels ("Field notes"); weather/locale strips ("LIS 14:23 · 18°C"); live
counters ("Reservation 412 of 800"); hero-bottom decoration strips ("BRAND. MOTION. SPATIAL.");
scroll cues ("Scroll to explore"); typewriter/rotator headlines and decorative pill badges; glassmorphism standing in for content; sparkle or aurora glows used as substance; emoji or Unicode glyphs standing in for an icon set, or mixed icon
families; sparklines, rings or tallies standing in for content; hover that moves the target or its
neighbours. **Invented metrics** (fabricated `92%`/`4.1×`/`48k`) — a stat is never the hero's sole
headline; use a real number or `—` + a labeled block. **Re-drawn chrome** (fake browser bars, phone
frames, code windows) — use a real screenshot. Meta-criticism copy and the other copy tells:
content.md.

## Calibration: the current AI cluster

Looks that recur regardless of subject — each legitimate for *some* brief, so treat it as a default
to justify, never a destination to drift into:

1. Warm cream `~#F4F1EA` + serif display + terracotta accent — including the "tasteful default" of a thin serif with one italic accent word and pill buttons, which looks considered and is why every generator lands on it.
2. Near-black + a single acid-green/vermilion accent.
3. Broadsheet: hairline rules, zero radius, dense columns — often plus an italic display serif and
   tracked mono labels.
4. Flat saturated ink, black boxes, hard offset shadows, heavy condensed display.
5. The SaaS-card kit: content chopped into identical rounded cards, one radius regardless of
   hierarchy, the same soft grey shadow, gradient washes as decoration.

**This model's measured prior:** warm, bookish, family or child subjects drift to cream, italic serif
and "lamplight" unasked. Start from the subject's saturated materials (bookcloth, thread, jackets,
endpapers); choose cream only when the contract names paper as the world. A pinned world pins
the world, not its softest rendition.

## Templated-design diagnosis and system fixes

Models default to the statistically common pattern (hero + three feature cards + testimonials +
pricing + CTA, Inter, indigo-600/slate-900, even spacing, stock imagery) because it is the average of
a billion tutorials. Fixes are **system-level**, not cosmetic: kill the default font; kill the default
colour and ration ONE accent; break the symmetric grid once; vary section rhythm; use real content;
add one or two intentional micro-interactions; and above all **feed references, not adjectives** —
"make it premium" yields the average; "a 1970s university lecture handout" steers the whole system.

**Second-order rebound.** Avoid the predictable anti-default rebound (fintech-not-navy → terminal
dark; wellness-not-sage → brutalist mono). The rebound is as templated as what it flees.
