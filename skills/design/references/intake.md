# Intake

Purpose: Scope a design request that arrives with no brief — infer what can be found, ask only what
the owner knows, reveal direction by showing options, and decide on the record when answers are thin.

Read when:
- A new surface, redesign or brand with no brief, direction contract or `digital-product/` context.
- The owner gives feedback in plain words ("make it pop") that has to become a design move.

Skip when:
- A tweak inside an established surface, or a contract already exists — go to the craft reference.
- The request is product discovery (who, what problem, what success) and `product` is running.

Inputs:
- The request, the repo and any existing site, screenshots, logo, copy, assets and LEDGER.

Produces:
- A brief block — Product · Audience · Action · Stakes · Assets · References and anti-references ·
  Constraints · Success — plus an assumption ledger. The design read is written from it; the chosen
  direction becomes LANE + DIMENSIONS + DEPARTS, each value citing a brief line or ledger row.

## Contents

- Owner questions

## Signals

Infer, don't ask; a fact you can find is never a question.

| Signal | Infer |
|---|---|
| The noun (dashboard, landing page, portfolio) | Product type and register → conventions.md row |
| Category (law, health, finance, kids, public sector) | Stakes dial, quiet constraints, proof leads |
| How the user writes (formal, slang, emoji, length) | Tone, and the words to mirror back |
| A logo or brand file | Palette anchor, shape language, era; equity to keep |
| An existing site or app | Change mode, IA, content inventory |
| Supplied photos or video and their quality | Imagery: photo-led only if the assets can carry it |
| "Like X", named competitors | Craft bar; codes to keep or break |
| Stack in the repo (component library, native) | Chrome convention; serve register likely |
| Locale and language | Script, text expansion, colour meaning |
| "Quick", "MVP", "for a pitch" | Depth: category standard, few departures |
| Price point | Premium ↔ information dial |

**Look at real references** (with web or image access): capture three to five competitors' first
screens and three references from outside the category; for each, write the one thing to keep, break
or borrow, and take departures from what no competitor owns. A direction built only from memory says
so in the ledger. **Rank the assets** the brief really has (a photograph, a real number, a quote, the
product at its best moment, the name); the first viewport is built on the strongest.

## Owner questions

One round, at most four, in the owner's words, each with a recommended answer inferred from the
signals ("ok" accepts all); never a style, palette or font. 1. What is it, and the one thing a visitor
should do? 2. Who is it for, and what must they believe before they act? 3. What exists: logo,
colours, type, photos, video, copy, screenshots, a current site? 4. One to three things you like and
one you don't want to resemble, from any field. A fifth only when the signals could not answer it:
what is fixed (platform, languages, brand rules, accessibility or legal requirements, deadline)?

## Direction cards

Show three named directions that differ in composition, not tint, on the same content: the category
standard, your recommendation, a bolder one. Plain words only, one card each:

> **B — "Ledger" (recommended)** · *Idea:* the site reads like the firm's own account book. · *Looks
> like:* white paper, black ink, one green used only for figures; a narrow serif; tables instead of
> cards; one engraved-style drawing. · *Feels:* exact, calm. · *Sits beside:* a private bank's annual
> report. · *Needs:* nothing new. · *Risk:* may read austere to younger clients.

The three differ on ≥3 axes including composition; each states what it needs (photographs, an
illustrator, a licence); never show one you would not build. Mixing is by axis ("B's layout, C's
colour"), never averaged. Before committing, sketch six or more first-viewport arrangements as boxes;
the first is the predicted one. Where you can render, each card is one first-viewport tile at one
size. For axes the cards leave open, at most four this-or-that pairs: calm ↔ energetic · spacious ↔
packed · classic ↔ of the moment · soft ↔ sharp · playful ↔ serious · real photos ↔ drawn ↔ words
only · light ↔ dark · familiar ↔ surprising.

## Vocabulary bridge

| They say | Set | Ask, if unclear |
|---|---|---|
| clean, simple, minimal | airy, Restrained, flat, few elements | "Like a gallery, or like a timetable?" |
| modern | nothing yet | "Modern compared with what?" |
| professional, trustworthy | convention, neutral ground, quiet type, proof early | — |
| premium, luxury | very airy, small type, restrained colour, photo-led, slow motion | — |
| fun, friendly | round shapes, Committed palette, illustration, bounce | "For adults or for children?" |
| bold, edgy | type-as-image, high contrast, hard edges | — |
| warm, human | people in photos, warm neutrals, humanist type, soft radius | — |
| techy | dark, dense, mono accents — a costume unless the audience is technical | "Who is the reader?" |
| calm · elegant | low chroma, slow motion, airy · high-contrast serif, hairlines | — |

## Assumption ledger

Thin answers: decide as the expert and record it — assumption · basis · cost to reverse (*low* =
tokens and copy; *medium* = imagery, a type licence; *high* = IA, concept, brand marks) · status. For
a high-cost assumption take the conservative option, or build so it swaps (tokens and lane in one
place). **Stop rule:** direction is decidable when register and product type, audience, primary
action, content reality and one reference or chosen direction are known or assumed on the record —
two rounds at most before something is shown.

## Reaction round and feedback

Show the cheapest proof — the first viewport plus one inner section — and ask: what do you notice
first? too loud or too quiet, too formal or too casual? what is missing? Diagnose feedback; never apply
the prescription literally: "make it pop" → weak hierarchy: more scale and value contrast on one thing,
not more colour everywhere · "cleaner" → too many things compete: remove and align, not just space ·
"more modern" → one dated signal: find and change it · "more premium" → too loud or full: fewer
elements, smaller type, less colour · "more fun" → shape, colour, motion, voice · "more professional"
→ alignment, one family, convention · "bigger logo" → the brand isn't felt: carry it in colour, type
and voice · "I don't like it" → show two alternatives that differ on one axis.

## Headless and no-brand

**No way to ask:** never block. Take the product type's convention with one to three departures
derived from the subject's own world, never from chance, so the same request yields the same
direction; invent no brand assets and no facts (honesty); keep the build reversible; open the artifact
with the ledger and the three answers that would change it most, and list every assumption in
`handoff.yaml` `assumptions` and `unresolved`. **No brand exists:** fix a minimum identity before the
page and record it as the DESIGN.md seed — the name as a typeset wordmark (no symbol invented), one
colour to own, one graphic device, an image rule, one voice line.
